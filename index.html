OLIVE: Joint Analysis and Synthesis for Self-Supervised Speech Representations

Karl El Hajal and Mathew Magimai-Doss

Motivation

Hearing and speaking

Speech communication fundamentally arises from perception (hearing) and production (speaking).

Analysis–synthesis

Classical speech processing embraced this through the analysis–synthesis paradigm.

Atal and Hanauer 1971 · McAulay and Quatieri 1986

Speech SSL

Speech SSL learns representations for predictive tasks, without explicitly preserving signal detail for generation. Synthesis remains peripheral.

Can we train one representation that supports both analysis and synthesis?

Challenges

Reconstruction requires signal-level information such as speaker characteristics and fine-grained acoustic detail.

Discriminative SSL benefits from representations that abstract away such variability.

Existing approaches

data2vec-SG

Reconstruction is a lightly weighted auxiliary loss on the top contextual layers. The decoder is discarded after pre-training.

Wang et al. 2023

UniWav

Jointly trains an encoder and a generative decoder. Its main limitation is lower recognition performance than SSL models of similar scale.

Liu et al. 2025

Typical Speech SSL Model Architecture

SSL Training Paradigms

Joint embedding

Pull the embeddings of two views of the same input together, in embedding space.

Example: BYOL-A

Generative

Predict the hidden part of the input from the visible part.

Examples: wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, WavLM

Joint embedding predictive

Predict the embedding of the hidden part from the visible part, in embedding space.

Examples: WavJEPA, data2vec

OLIVE combines masked latent prediction and waveform reconstruction

Functional Separation

Synthesis encourages early features to maintain signal-level detail.
Analysis shapes the later layers.
Both objectives share one encoder and one pre-training stage.

Downstream tasks

SUPERB evaluates frozen SSL models as general-purpose feature extractors.

For each task, a learned weighted sum of all encoder layers is fed to a lightweight task head.

  • Content: speech recognition, keyword spotting
  • Speaker: identification, verification, diarization
  • Paralinguistic: emotion recognition
  • Semantic: intent classification, slot filling, speech translation
  • Generation: speech enhancement, source separation, voice conversion

Waveform reconstruction

Train the same HiFi-GAN V2 on each model's frozen local features.

Evaluate on LibriSpeech test-clean.

Also evaluate each OLIVE-J model's integrated vocoder.

  • Quality: PESQ, ViSQOL, UTMOS
  • Intelligibility: STOI, WER
  • Speaker similarity: SECS
  • Spectral fidelity: Mel-L1, LSD, MCD
  • Pitch: F₀ MAE
  • Signal fidelity: SI-SDR, SNR

Conclusions

Reconstruction on early features and masked prediction on later ones can be trained together in a single pre-training stage.
Reconstruction improves resynthesis and speaker information; recognition stays close to data2vec 2.0.
Waveform augmentations shift the representation toward speaker and generation tasks, at a small recognition cost.
The vocoder is kept after pre-training and reconstructs waveforms.

Limitations

  • English only
  • LibriSpeech 960 h, read speech
  • Base size only
